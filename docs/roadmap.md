# Where this is going

What this project is for, what comes next and in what order, and what has been put aside and why. The decisions already taken, with the alternatives they beat, are in [design.md](design.md) section 2; what shipped is in the [changelog](../CHANGELOG.md). The rules for working on the code are in [CONTRIBUTING.md](../CONTRIBUTING.md).

## The goal

A TiddlyWiki anyone hosts on Cloudflare's free plan in about ten minutes, from a fork of this repository as it is: nothing edited in the fork, the settings in the dashboard, updates by syncing the fork. The wiki runs on Cloudflare only; GitHub keeps the backup (one commit per save) and the code. Behind Cloudflare Access it's private; given a domain, it's also a public site that costs no worker requests to read.

## Who it's for

1. **A public wiki** — a blog, documentation, notes you share — edited on a private admin site. What the project does today; its setup by hand checked from the wiki (done: [operating 3.15](operating.md#315-checking-the-setup)).
2. **A private notebook** — the admin site only, behind Access, no domain to own, edited offline, opened offline, installed as an app (done: [setup 2.1](setup.md#21-before-you-start), [operating 3.11](operating.md#311-editing-offline), [3.13](operating.md#313-opening-the-wiki-offline) and [3.16](operating.md#316-installing-the-wiki-as-an-app)).
3. **Both at once, or one then the other:** a private wiki that later gets a public site, by adding its pieces, the domain, the rules and the settings, with nothing to migrate (done, [design 2.15](design.md#215-a-private-wiki-is-the-same-worker-without-a-public-address), [operating 3.22](operating.md#322-making-a-private-wiki-public)).

## Next, in order

Each step is one pull request, with its tests, its page in the manual, its design entry and its changelog line. "Done when" is written as behaviour, and becomes the list of tests when the step starts.

The steps the goal needed are done. The last of them, a Deploy to Cloudflare button (2026-10-19, the day the repository went public), was taken out again on 2026-10-26: its copy wasn't a fork, so every update needed a script, and the fork with *Sync fork* is the one path ([design 2.30](design.md#230-the-deploy-to-cloudflare-button-three-files-the-variables-kept-out-of-them)). What comes next comes from use: an idea goes under Parked with its reason, or to an issue first. Three steps are scheduled, from use (2026-10-27):

### 1. A reader's copy kept: persistent storage on the public site

**Why now:** a wiki that grows big and whose readers want it whole with no network, the notes of a tabletop campaign at the table, say. The public site opens offline since [design 2.40](design.md#240-the-public-site-offline-the-same-service-worker-from-the-bucket-the-page-stale-while-revalidating), and the fill, on by default and a setting saved in the wiki, so the public site follows it ([operating 3.10](operating.md#310-loading-a-big-wiki)), brings every tiddler to a reader's device. But a browser short of space clears a site's storage whole, the service worker's cache and the device copy together, and the wiki's answer to that, *Ask the browser to keep this site's data* in the device tab ([operating 3.18](operating.md#318-this-device)), exists on the admin site alone: the device tab and the outbox module don't run on the public site. A reader has no way to ask.

**Done when:**
- On the public site, the cloud icon's dropdown offers to keep this site's data on this device, beside *Log in to edit* ([design 2.41](design.md#241-the-public-sites-cloud-icon-log-in-to-edit-and-the-offline-line)): asked on a click, never at page load, for the reason of [design 2.22](design.md#222-the-device-tab-measured-on-demand-the-promise-asked-on-a-click-the-copy-forgotten-from-the-wiki) (Firefox puts the question to the user; Chrome and Safari answer by their own rules). The admin site keeps the tab's button, nothing more.
- The item then says what the browser answered, in the device tab's words: kept, or best effort and what decides it (a site used often, installed or bookmarked; in Safari the installed app alone). At a page load only what was promised so far is read, no question asked.
- A reader who got the promise keeps the page, the engine, the plugins and every tiddler the fill brought, through the browser's cleanups; without it, nothing changes from today.
- No worker request, nothing per save or per load: the public site still costs none.
- The manual: the readers' paragraph of [operating 3.13](operating.md#313-opening-the-wiki-offline) says how to keep the copy and what the answer means; the design entry says why a click and why the dropdown; the public-offline suite covers the item on the public site, its absence on the admin site, and the answer shown as the browser gives it.

### 2. The setup check links the repositories, and the token form filled in

**Why now:** asked while setting up a demo wiki (2026-10-27). *Check the setup* names the backup repository as text and links its commits only on the passing line; it never links the repository the worker is deployed from; and where it says to make a GitHub token, it links GitHub's token list, while the form already filled in (the name, the description, a year, *Contents: read and write*) is in [setup 2.4](setup.md#24-the-backup-repository-optional) alone.

**Done when:**
- Every backups line that names `GITHUB_REPO`, failing or passing, links the repository itself; the passing line keeps its link to the commits.
- Every line that says to make a token links GitHub's form filled in, as setup 2.4's link, the owner of `GITHUB_REPO` filled in too when GitHub's form takes it (its parameters checked against GitHub's documentation first); the token list stays the link for regenerating one.
- The check links the repository the worker was deployed from. Open question before starting: the worker doesn't know it at run time; Workers Builds gives the build the commit and the branch (to check: whether it gives the repository's name), which `npm run build` could bake in; without it, a setting, or the project's own repository with the release line's link ([design 2.32](design.md#232-the-update-available-line-github-asked-from-the-browser-when-the-tab-is-shown-no-button-no-setting)).
- No new request: the links come from the settings and the build; the repository suite checks each new address is listed in reference 7, as the check's other links are.

### 3. The plugin's words cut down, the interface carrying the state

**Why now:** the owner's own reading, setting up a demo wiki (2026-10-27): the plugin explains in sentences what its interface could show. The Cloudflare tab, *Check the setup*, the device tab, the cloud icon's dropdown and the alerts each give the state, the reason, the consequence and the manual's section in one paragraph, which reads as documentation inside the wiki; the explanations belong in the manual, which every line links already.

**Done when:**
- First, an inventory of every text the plugin shows (the modules under `src/browser/`, the plugin's tiddlers, the setup check's lines in `src/worker/setup.js`), each with what it must tell at a glance; the plan, in the user's words, approved before any rewrite (CONTRIBUTING).
- Each line leads with its state as the interface shows it (✓, –, ✗, a badge, a button's own label), then one short sentence, at most, of what to do; the reason and the background are behind the manual's link or a disclosure, not in the line.
- Nothing a user needs to act is lost: what to do, where, and the section with the full steps stay one click away; the alerts keep what they must say in the moment (a change kept on the device, a login expired).
- The suites that match the plugin's words are updated with it, checking what each line means (its state, its action, its link) rather than its exact sentence where they can; the README's screenshot regenerated.
- No new request, no new setting; the plugin no heavier than today.

## Parked

Ideas with a reason to wait. Each says what it would be if it came back, so the reason can be checked rather than the idea rediscovered.

- **Static pages** (for search engines and readers without JavaScript, as tiddlywiki.com offers): rendered by the admin wiki, which already renders the page, into the bucket; not GitHub Pages, not an Action in the backup repository (the wiki stays on Cloudflare). Parked: not needed for the first audiences; the public wiki is a full TiddlyWiki already.
- **Several wikis on one worker:** today a second wiki is a second worker, from the same repository with Wrangler environments ([setup 2.9](setup.md#29-a-second-wiki)). The argument for one worker is one update for every wiki. If it comes back: a wiki per hostname, each with its own bucket or prefix and settings; a session's work, and it changes the storage layout, so it waits for a need.
- **A setup wizard** — a web page doing the setup through Cloudflare's API with a token — parked until the setup by hand proves too much for the audience; the Deploy to Cloudflare button, tried for a week in October 2026, wasn't the answer ([design 2.30](design.md#230-the-deploy-to-cloudflare-button-three-files-the-variables-kept-out-of-them)).
- **Image and file upload** from the admin wiki into the bucket, served by the public site, with a tiddler pointing at the file (`_canonical_uri`). Parked: not asked for yet; noted when the missing features were reviewed.
- **Log out and forget this device,** an entry beside *Logout* in the cloud icon's dropdown: Forget's refusals (something waits, the worker doesn't answer), then the copy, the outbox and the service worker's files removed, then Access's logout page instead of a reload; and the service worker answering a *Logout* tapped with no network with a line ("No network: Logout needs the network to reach Access") rather than the cached page, which reloads the wiki in place. Device-local, like Forget ([design 2.22](design.md#222-the-device-tab-measured-on-demand-the-promise-asked-on-a-click-the-copy-forgotten-from-the-wiki)); nothing account-level. Parked: [setup 2.6](setup.md#26-cloudflare-access) says what Logout leaves on the device and what to do on a computer that isn't yours (a private window; or log out, then clear the site's data), which covers it until someone asks.
- **The replaced engine kept,** as `_page/index-previous.html` when *Replace it* writes the shipped one over `index.html`, with a put-back button in the tab: one get and one put per engine update. Parked until asked for: the ask says the swap can't be undone and to download the current `index.html` first ([operating 3.3](operating.md#33-updating-the-wiki-engine)), and the worker puts its own engine back when `index.html` is deleted.
- **Restore in reverse** — a tool writing the backup straight into the bucket. Parked: the import covers it ([setup 2.4](setup.md#24-the-backup-repository-optional)), and it would need the tiddler list rebuilt afterwards.
- **A private site behind Access:** for a private wiki that has a domain, the bucket's custom domain protected by Cloudflare Access too, so the admin wiki reads tiddlers, the core file and plugin files through Cloudflare's cache with no worker request, as a public wiki does, while nobody else can. Needs the Access session shared across the two hostnames, reads sent with credentials (the CORS rule with `Allow-Credentials`), and the existing fallback to the worker for a read Access refuses. Open question before starting: whether a response Access allows is served from the cache rather than from R2 every time. Parked: a setup option for one audience. The private wiki and the cold start it waited for are done (checked 2026-10-17); what still comes first is the step above, and the open question wants an answer from a real zone before any code.

- **Batch reads from the worker** (a private wiki only): a `POST /batch` route answering a list of titles with the stored objects streamed as one JSON array, no parsing (each is a JSON document already), plugged into the list sync's read-ahead, which would fetch the next 50 or so in one request instead of 6 single ones. What it saves: worker requests, a thousand-tiddler first load on a new device going from 1,000 to about 20, and the same for catching up after a long offline stretch. What it doesn't: R2 reads (no multi-get: a batch of 50 is 50 reads), bandwidth, and anything on a public wiki, whose reads come from Cloudflare's cache without the worker. First step if it comes back: measure, with the CPU suite, how many R2 reads and how many bytes one request can pass through under the 10 ms budget; that number is the batch size, and decides whether it's worth having. Parked: a thousand requests once per device, against 100,000 a day, doesn't bite for one person, and `LoadAll = no` already cuts it to what's shown; worth it when a private wiki is used from many devices or by several people, or when someone hits the daily cap.
- **Miniflare 5 for the tests,** once it's out of beta (checked 2026-10-19: the last Miniflare 4 is from 2026-07-30, and 5 is in beta). It declares the `undici` and `sharp` versions that `package.json` pins by override today ([development 6.2](development.md#62-the-tests)), so the pin goes with the move; a major version, so the browser harness and every suite are checked against it first. Parked: a beta isn't what the tests should depend on.
- **The browser modules' comments left out of the plugin the worker builds into the page:** the modules ship as their source, 200 KB with their comments, and the admin page is about 550 KB against the engine's 2.8 MB (the page-publish and private suites check it stays under a quarter). Parked: the page is loaded once per device and cached; worth doing when its size is felt, or when the modules' texts grow much more.
- **Live sync, pushed through the worker:** a Durable Object per wiki holding a WebSocket to each open admin tab, hibernating between messages; a save or delete the worker handles tells it what changed, and the tabs take the change as they take one from another tab of the same browser today ([design 1.16](design.md#116-tabs-following-each-other)), so a change shows on every open device at once, and connected tabs stop polling: fewer worker requests, not more. Not peer-to-peer: two browsers on different networks would need a signalling server (this one) plus STUN and often a TURN relay, both devices open at the same moment, and the worker for the save anyway, since R2 is the store of record. Open before starting: the Durable Object's own accounting on the free plan (requests and duration counted apart from the worker's), Cloudflare Access on the socket, the outbox's interplay. Parked: for one person with two devices a change seen within the minute is rarely felt; worth it when a wiki is open on several devices at once, or when the polls' requests start to count (today 1,440 a day per visible tab, against 100,000).
- **Load them first, beside the snapshot line:** a button loading every tiddler not loaded yet before a snapshot, on the owner's click (on a private wiki one worker request per tiddler, what the fill costs). Parked: the fill does it when on ([operating 3.10](operating.md#310-loading-a-big-wiki)), and the line under the snapshot button says to wait for it or to turn it on ([operating 3.26](operating.md#326-your-wiki-as-one-file)).
- **A reload notice for a reader's open tab.** What a tab open on the public site gets by itself is content, through the list: tiddlers, the site title, a palette. What comes with the page it gets at a load: a plugin the owner adds (its file is a script tag of the page, [design 1.8](design.md#18-plugins-and-javascript-modules); in the open tab it arrives as a tiddler without text and does nothing, TiddlyWiki's own reload bar staying down since it judges a plugin by its text), raw markup, an engine update, this plugin's own updates. And with the page stale while revalidating ([design 2.40](design.md#240-the-public-site-offline-the-same-service-worker-from-the-bucket-the-page-stale-while-revalidating)) the next load shows the kept page and the one after the new, so a plugin reaches a reader two loads later, with no sign in between. If it comes back: the service worker tells its tabs when the page fetched behind differs from the kept one (the text, not a 304), the module raises TiddlyWiki's reload bar as the admin site does after a deploy ([design 2.21](design.md#221-the-modules-as-a-plugin-the-settings-in-its-tab-the-advanced-ones-under-a-warning)), and that reload is served from the network first, so one reload is enough; no request. Parked: for an owner who adds plugins or changes the shell often; a reader meets the new page at a later visit, and the step above comes first.
